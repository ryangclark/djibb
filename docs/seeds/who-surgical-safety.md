# WHO Surgical Safety Checklist

Nineteen checks at three pauses in an operation. Introduced by the World Health Organization in 2008 and popularized by Atul Gawande's *The Checklist Manifesto*, it cut surgical deaths and complications by over a third in the pilot hospitals. The WHO explicitly encourages teams to adapt it to local practice — a checklist is a living document, not a stone tablet.

## Sign in — before induction of anesthesia

- [ ] Patient has confirmed identity, site, procedure, and consent
- [ ] Surgical site is marked (or marking not applicable)
- [ ] Anesthesia machine and medication check complete
- [ ] Pulse oximeter is on the patient and functioning
- [ ] Known allergies reviewed
- [ ] Difficult airway or aspiration risk assessed, equipment ready
- [ ] Risk of major blood loss assessed, IV access and fluids planned

## Time out — before skin incision

- [ ] All team members have introduced themselves by name and role
- [ ] Patient's name, procedure, and incision site confirmed aloud
- [ ] Antibiotic prophylaxis given within the last 60 minutes (or not indicated)
- [ ] Surgeon reviews critical steps, expected duration, anticipated blood loss
- [ ] Anesthesia team reviews patient-specific concerns
- [ ] Nursing team confirms sterility and raises any equipment issues
- [ ] Essential imaging is displayed

## Sign out — before the patient leaves the operating room

- [ ] Name of the procedure recorded
- [ ] Instrument, sponge, and needle counts are complete
- [ ] Specimens labelled and read aloud, including patient name
- [ ] Equipment problems noted for follow-up
- [ ] Team reviews key concerns for recovery
