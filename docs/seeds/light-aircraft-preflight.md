# Light Aircraft Preflight

The checklist's origin story: in 1935 Boeing's B-17 crashed on a demonstration flight because one step was forgotten, and test pilots invented the pre-flight checklist rather than accept that the plane was "too much airplane for one man to fly." Every pilot since has run one before every flight, no matter how many thousand hours they have. Adapted from a typical Cessna 172 flow — real pilots: use your aircraft's POH.

## Cabin

- [ ] Documents aboard — airworthiness, registration, operating handbook
- [ ] Control lock removed
- [ ] Master switch on, fuel quantity checked, master off
- [ ] Fuel — 0/40 gal
- [ ] Oil — 0/8 qt

## Walkaround

- [ ] Control surfaces free, hinges and linkages secure
- [ ] Fuel sumps drained and sampled — 0/5 points
- [ ] Pitot tube cover removed, static ports clear
- [ ] Tires inflated, no cuts; brakes not leaking
- [ ] Propeller and spinner free of nicks and cracks
- [ ] Baggage secured, doors latched

## Before takeoff

- [ ] Seats, belts, and doors — adjusted and locked
- [ ] Flight controls — free and correct
- [ ] Engine run-up complete, instruments in the green
- [ ] Trim and flaps set for takeoff
- [ ] Departure briefing — runway, abort point, emergency plan
